Work stuff

Voice Receptionist

An AI that answers a business's phone and books appointments, like a person at the front desk would. It checks the real calendar, remembers people who called before, takes messages, and hands the call to a human when it should.

The hard part was never the AI talking. It was time: staff hours, buffers, daylight saving, two people trying to book the same slot.

still working on it — most of it happened Sep–Oct.

This one's private, but ask me and I'll show you how it works.

A CRM with a voice agent inside

Software for small service businesses: bookings, dispatch, billing. There's an AI in it that sorts what customers ask for into six careful paths — booking, changes, warranty, new jobs, follow-ups, or “let me get a human”.

I spent a lot of time on tests here, because when it's someone's real business you really don't want the agent improvising.

built for a client, so no name. May was a crazy month (73 commits).

Private — happy to walk you through it.

A data assistant for a team (confidential)

People ask questions about their numbers in plain words and it answers from their SQL database.

The thing I'm proud of is boring: I don't let the AI do the math. The calculations are normal code that's checked; the AI only explains the result. Language models are confident about numbers they made up.

Can't show this one, sorry. I can tell you how it's built.

Another one generates different designs and has a strict “critic” judge them — this site was designed with it, and then I ignored half of what the critic said.

Play stuff

Emberquill

A little 3D tabletop for D&D-style fights, in the browser. Rules, enemy AI, a map builder, a character creator, and online battles with a human Dungeon Master. You can throw furniture.

I got completely obsessed — 326 commits in under three weeks.

It's live — go break something.

BookForge

A place for writers to keep their whole story world straight — who knows what, who's where, which plot threads are still open.

The rule I care about: the AI is an editor, not a ghostwriter. It points out “this character couldn't know that yet”, but it never writes your book.

still building. Local-first, your book stays on your computer.

Kana Notebook

A small app for learning Japanese, in English and Russian: hiragana, words, grammar, review. Students can ask for things that are missing and vote on them. It's free.

It's live.
